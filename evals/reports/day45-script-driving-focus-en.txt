[preface / curated]
Before we begin, a note about safety. Please do not use this practice while driving. If you are driving, pull over somewhere safe and stop before you begin this. It is for when you are stationary. You can stop at any time, and this is a self-guided practice, not medical advice and not treatment.

[stop / curated]
You can stop at any time, and you can let go of any instruction that does not suit you.

[eyes / curated]
You can keep your eyes open if you like, resting your gaze somewhere quiet in front of you.

[opening / curated]
This is a short pause for your attention. We are not trying to clear your mind that is not possible, and it is not the point. We are just giving your attention a place to rest.

[passage / curated]
You will notice that your attention wanders. That is normal. When you notice it has gone, you bring it back, gently, without criticism. That small return is the whole practice.

[passage / curated]
Let your gaze be soft. Let your hands rest. Let the rest of the day wait a while it has been waiting this long, and it can wait a little more.

[breath / curated]
Notice one breath at a time. In, and out. You are welcome to let your attention wander, and you are welcome back to the breath when it comes.

[passage / curated]
Bring your attention to the sound of your own breathing. You do not need to change it just let it be the thing you are paying attention to, for a few breaths.

[passage / curated]
If a worry comes by, you can give it a name in your mind a to-do, a message, a person and then let it be. Naming it often gives it enough attention that it can rest.

[breath / curated]
Let us take a few ordinary breaths together. In through the nose, and out through the mouth, whenever that feels natural. There is no pace to match.

[closing / curated]
One more breath in, and out. When you return to what you were doing, you are allowed to do it at a pace that feels kind. You can stop now, whenever you are ready.
