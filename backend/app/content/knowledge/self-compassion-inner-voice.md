Listen to how you speak to yourself after a mistake. Now imagine saying the
same words, in the same tone, to a friend who just made the same mistake:
"You're hopeless." "You always ruin things." You would never. It would be
cruel, and it would not even help them.

The person you spend the most time with is you. The voice you use indoors,
in your own head, shapes how hard everything feels.

Kindness is not letting yourself off the hook. Research on self-compassion
keeps finding the same thing: people who treat themselves kindly try again
sooner, own mistakes more honestly, and bounce back faster — because they
are not spending their energy on a second layer of shame.

A simple three-part way to answer your inner critic:

1. **Acknowledge:** "That was hard. I'm feeling ashamed right now."
2. **Common humanity:** "Other people go through this too. I'm not the only
   one, and I'm not the worst one."
3. **Kind, useful next line:** "What would help now?" — then do that small
   thing: water, rest, one step, telling someone.

Try literally asking: "What would I say to my friend in this exact
situation?" — then say that, to you. If it feels unnatural at first, that is
normal. You are learning a dialect. Fluency comes with use.
