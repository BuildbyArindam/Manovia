Keeping everything inside feels safe, but it is expensive: the same worry,
carried alone for months, gets heavier, not lighter. Telling one trusted
person does not fix the problem — it shares the weight, and shared weight is
carryable.

Who to choose: someone who has shown they can listen without making it about
themselves. It does not have to be family. A friend, a sibling, a cousin, a
teacher, a mentor, a faith leader. Someone who is kind and, ideally, someone
who has shown they can keep things private.

Getting started is the hardest ten seconds. You do not need the perfect
words:

- "Can I tell you something? I've not been doing so great lately."
- "I need to say something out loud. You don't have to fix it."
- "I've been carrying this alone for a while and I'm tired of it."

After the first sentence, honesty does most of the work. Say the plain
version, not the polished one: "I feel alone." "I can't sleep most nights."
"I keep pretending I'm fine."

It is okay to ask for what you need: "I don't want advice, I just wanted to
say it." It is also okay if it comes out messy or with tears. Messy is how
true things usually arrive.

If the first person responds badly, that is about their limits, not your
worth — and it is worth trying once more with someone else. And if there is
no one in reach right now, a helpline listener will do for tonight; the
button in this app can show you one.
