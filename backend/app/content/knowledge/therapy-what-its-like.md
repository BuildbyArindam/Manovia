If you have never been to therapy, the room in your imagination is probably
stranger than the real one. Here is what it is usually like.

**It is a conversation, not an examination.** You talk; they listen, ask
questions, and help you see your own situation more clearly. There is no
couch you must lie on, no test to pass, no wrong answers.

**The first session is mostly questions.** What brings you in, how long it
has been going on, how you sleep, eat, study, who is in your life. You
control the pace — "I'm not ready to talk about that yet" is a completely
respectable sentence there.

**They do not judge, and they have heard worse.** Whatever you are carrying,
it sits comfortably within the job. Saying the unsayable to a trained
listener, and not being dropped, is a large part of what makes it work.

**It is confidential**, with narrow exceptions a good therapist explains up
front (roughly: serious risk to your safety or someone else's). You can and
should ask "what happens to what I say?" — a good one answers clearly.

**It is work, and it is gradual.** Sessions often go for under an hour,
weekly or fortnightly, for a stretch of weeks. Some sessions are tiring;
many people leave lighter. Progress looks like slightly better weeks, not
lightning.

**You are allowed to shop.** Fit matters enormously. If you do not feel
respected or safe with one therapist, trying another is normal practice,
not rudeness.

Therapy is not for "people who can't cope". It is for people who would
rather not cope alone — which is most people, at some point.
