Loneliness is one of the most common feelings there is, and one of the
hardest to admit. If you are feeling it, you are in a very large company of
people who also feel it.

The hard part: loneliness whispers "stay hidden; reaching out is risky" —
but hiding is the thing that feeds it. The way through is not one grand
friendship. It is a series of small, low-pressure contacts that slowly
re-open the door.

Some low-pressure ways to make contact:

- **A one-line message.** "Saw this and thought of you." No ask, no agenda,
  no essay. You can end a one-line conversation any time.
- **Being around people without talking.** A library, a park, a place of
  worship, a café. Parallel company is real company.
- **A repeating, structured thing.** A class, a club, volunteering, a sports
  group. Regularity does the work: you see the same faces, and familiarity
  does the talking for the first weeks.
- **Replying to the person who already texted you.** The bridge that exists
  is easier than the bridge you have to build.

And be fair to yourself about speed. Connection is slow farming, not fast
food. One message today, one small "how are you?" this week — that is how
the fabric re-knits.

If loneliness has been sitting on your chest for weeks and everything feels
pointless, please tell someone — a helpline exists exactly for evenings like
that, and talking to one person is not a small thing. It is the whole thing.
