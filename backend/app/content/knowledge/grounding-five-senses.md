When your thoughts are spinning, it can help to give your attention something
concrete to hold. This exercise walks your senses from the room back to you.

Slowly notice:

- **5 things you can see.** Small ones count: a chip in the table, a fold in
  your sleeve.
- **4 things you can feel.** Your feet on the floor, fabric on your skin, the
  air on your face.
- **3 things you can hear.** Near sounds first, then distant ones.
- **2 things you can smell**, or two smells you like.
- **1 thing you can taste**, even just a sip of water.

There is no wrong way to do this, and going out of order is fine. If you lose
count, start again anywhere. Some people find it helps to name each thing out
loud, slowly, like telling a friend what is in the room.

Grounding does not push feelings away. It gives you an anchor while feelings
move through, the way holding a rail helps on a moving bus.

If an exercise like this brings up strong or painful feelings, it is okay to
stop and try something gentler, like pressing both feet into the floor and
breathing out slowly.
