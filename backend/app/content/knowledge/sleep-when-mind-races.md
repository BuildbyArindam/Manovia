The moment the lights go off, the day's unfinished business lines up to be
felt. A racing mind at night is exhausting — and very common.

Things that can take the pressure off:

- **Write it down before bed.** Keep paper by the bed. One line per worry, in
  the ugliest handwriting you like. The page can hold it until morning; you
  do not have to solve anything at midnight.
- **Give the thought a place to wait.** Some people picture a shelf, a
  drawer, or a queue. The worry is filed, not followed.
- **Give your mind a boring job.** Count breaths backwards from ten, name
  words that start with a letter, or listen to something slow and quiet. A
  mind occupied with something gentle has less room to rehearse.
- **Drop the fight.** Watching the clock and insisting "I must sleep now"
  keeps you awake. Letting go of the deadline often lets sleep creep back.

One bad night will not undo you, even though everything feels heavier at 3
a.m. Nights like this pass. If most nights are like this for weeks, it is
worth telling someone — a doctor or a counsellor can help with sleep that
will not settle.
