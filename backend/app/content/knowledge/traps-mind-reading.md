Mind reading is deciding what someone else is thinking without checking. We
all do it, and we almost always guess worse than reality.

It sounds like:

- "She replied slowly. She's annoyed with me."
- "They didn't invite me because they think I'm boring."
- "The teacher's expression means I disappointed them."

Two things are usually wrong with the guess: we assume we are the cause of
their mood (people are mostly thinking about their own lives), and we assume
the interpretation is the worst one available.

Ways to loosen it:

- **Count the other explanations.** Slow reply: busy, driving, battery,
  thinking, or — genuinely — annoyed. Five options; you picked the one that
  hurts most and called it a fact.
- **Separate observation from story.** "The message sat unanswered for two
  hours" is an observation. "She hates me" is a story you wrote.
- **Check it, gently, if it matters.** "Hey, all okay? Your reply seemed
  short." One sentence of checking beats hours of guessing. If you cannot
  ask, practise saying: "I don't actually know what they think. I'm
  guessing."
- **Give them the same slack you'd want.** If you were quiet today, would
  you want a friend concluding you disliked them?

Nobody can actually read minds — including yours, which is worth remembering
when you assume you know what they're thinking about you.
