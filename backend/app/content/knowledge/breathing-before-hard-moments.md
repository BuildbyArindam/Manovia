There are moments you cannot skip — walking into an exam, unmuting on a call,
knocking on a teacher's door. A short breathing routine in the two minutes
before can steady you enough to walk in as yourself.

Before you go in:

- Two slow breaths out, each longer than the breath in. Let the last of the
  air leave, without forcing.
- On the final breath out, drop your shoulders an inch and unclench your jaw.
- Give yourself one plain sentence: "I have prepared as much as I could" or
  simply "One thing at a time."

That is the whole routine. It is not magic; it lowers the volume a notch, and
a notch is often all you need to start.

If nerves surge *during* the moment, anchor your feet on the floor and take
one slow, quiet breath out before continuing. Nobody will notice, and you can
repeat it as often as you like.

Remember that some nervousness is not your enemy. A raised heart rate can be
your body getting ready to do the thing. You do not have to feel calm to do
it well — you only have to begin.
