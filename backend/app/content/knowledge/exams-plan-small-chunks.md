"I have to study everything" is a thought that crushes you before you start.
You do not have to study everything. You have to study the next small thing.

- **Pick one topic and one goal.** Not "revise biology" but "make one page of
  notes on cell division" or "do ten questions from last year's paper".
- **Set a timer, not a syllabus.** Twenty-five minutes of work, five minutes
  of real break. Two or three rounds, then a longer rest. The timer holds the
  commitment so you do not have to hold it in your head.
- **Write tomorrow's first task tonight.** One line. Future-you should be
  able to start without deciding anything.
- **Count what you did, not what is left.** Before bed, note what you
  finished. A visible list of done things fights the feeling that you are
  always behind.
- **Leave the hardest topic for a fresh hour**, not the hour you have left at
  midnight.

Progress you can see is calmer than progress you can't. Small chunks done
steadily beat heroic nights that leave you wrecked for two days.

And if studying has become impossible — sitting down but nothing goes in,
every day — that is not laziness. It is a sign of a load worth talking about
with someone.
