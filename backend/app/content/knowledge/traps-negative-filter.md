The filter takes a day with nine okay moments and one bad one, and hands you
only the bad one at the end. You can eat, laugh once, finish something, get a
kind message — and still close the day thinking "today was awful."

It sounds like:

- "Yes, but" — the but that erases everything before it.
- "That doesn't count" — the compliment from someone who likes you, the
  question you answered, the help you gave.
- "Anyone could have done that." Maybe. You did it; that is what counts.

The filter feels like honesty. It is actually a faulty search engine: it only
runs the query "what went wrong?", so it only ever finds negatives.

Rebalancing the search:

- **Three lines, nightly.** Before bed, write three things that were okay,
  fine, or less bad than feared — however small. You are not listing
  blessings; you are retraining what the search notices.
- **Let good things count once.** When someone is kind or you do something
  well, pause for ten seconds and let it register. Small moment, real
  effect.
- **Read the whole receipt.** Ask: "What went wrong, what went fine, and
  what went better than expected?" A day reviewed in full is usually
  painfully average — which is a far kinder verdict than "awful".
- **Watch "yes, but".** Try finishing the sentence without the but, once.

This is not about pretending the hard parts are fine. It is about seeing the
whole day, which you deserve to do.
