Walking out knowing it went badly is a particular kind of horrible — the
sinking stomach, the loops of "if only I had". For today, some things matter
more than analysis.

- **Let it be bad for a bit.** You are allowed to be upset without instantly
  turning it into a lesson. Feelings this fresh are not good advisors yet.
- **Postpone the verdict.** "It went terribly" is a first-draft thought, not
  a fact. Set a date with yourself to review it — after results, or in three
  days — and until then, refuse to grade the exam in your head.
- **Keep the basics running.** Food, a walk, people, sleep. Setbacks hit
  harder on an empty tank, and the basics are how the tank refills.
- **Tell one person the honest version.** Not the polished one. Shame shrinks
  when it is spoken.
- **When you are ready, mine it gently.** One or two useful questions: was it
  preparation, panic, timing, or the question itself? The answer changes what
  you do next time — that is all it needs to change.

A bad exam is a data point about one day. Courses have room in them for bad
days. You are allowed to take that room, and you are allowed to be treated
kindly — by others, and especially by you — while you work out what is next.
