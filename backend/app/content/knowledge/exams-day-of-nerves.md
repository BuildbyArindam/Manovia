Exam mornings can make your body feel like it is in trouble. It is not. It is
getting ready to perform. Some things that help it along:

- **Decide nothing new today.** Clothes out, bag packed, route known —
  ideally the night before. Every small decision you skip is calm you keep.
- **Eat something plain.** Nerves can shrink appetite; a little food beats
  none, and a familiar breakfast beats an experiment.
- **Caffeine in moderation.** If you are already jittery, more stimulant is
  not your friend. Water helps more.
- **Arrive early, not early-early.** Sitting outside the hall for an hour
  marinating in everyone's panic helps nobody. Ten or fifteen minutes of
  buffer is plenty.
- **Skip the corridor comparisons.** "I've learnt nothing" and "that was so
  easy" are both unreliable. Headphones, or a friend who agrees to talk about
  something else, protect your headspace.
- **One slow breath out before you open the paper.** Then start with
  something you can do, to warm up.

You do not need to feel calm to do this well. You only need to begin, one
question at a time.
