When a friend is struggling, the pull is to fix it fast. Most of the time,
what they need first is not your solution. It is your attention.

Listening that actually helps:

- **Give the moment room.** Put the phone face down. Turn towards them. Ten
  unhurried minutes beats an hour of distracted company.
- **Ask once, gently, and let silence work.** "You've seemed really low
  lately — how are you, honestly?" If they pause, wait. Silence says "you
  can say it".
- **Reflect before responding.** "That sounds exhausting." "So it's been
  building for weeks?" Being accurately heard is itself medicine.
- **Resist comparing.** Their pain does not need to be worse than someone
  else's to matter, and your story can wait for another day.
- **Don't argue them out of it.** "Don't think that" and "others have it
  worse" close doors. "That sounds really hard. I'm glad you told me" opens
  them.
- **End with a small next thing, together.** "Want to get food?" "Shall we
  talk again on Thursday?" One concrete, tiny plan beats a speech.

Take seriously anything that sounds like hopelessness, being a burden, or
not wanting to be here. You do not have to handle it alone or perfectly:
stay with them, and help them reach support — a trusted adult, a counsellor,
or a crisis helpline (the help button in this app lists one). If they seem
in immediate danger, stay with them and contact emergency help.

You are not their therapist, and you must not be. You are the friend. That
is already a lot, and it is exactly the right job.
