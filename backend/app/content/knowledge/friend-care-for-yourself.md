Standing by someone through a hard time is real work. It is work people do
out of love — and it can quietly empty you if you do it with no rest, no
limits, and the feeling that everything depends on you.

Some honest ground rules:

- **You are a friend, not a treatment plan.** You can listen, sit with them
  and walk them towards help. You cannot heal them, and it was never your
  job to.
- **You can care and still sleep.** Answering at 3 a.m. every night helps
  nobody by Thursday. "I can't talk now — I'll message you at lunch" is a
  kind sentence, not a betrayal.
- **Share the load where you can.** With their consent, loop in another
  friend, a family member, a teacher or a counsellor. One person holding one
  person alone is a fragile structure. Three people holding one person is a
  net.
- **Watch your own signals.** Dread when their name appears, your own sleep
  slipping, your marks or meals sliding — those are your metre running low.
  Refill with the same basics you'd offer them: rest, food, people, movement.
- **Get support for you, privately.** Talk to someone about how it feels to
  support them — without breaking their confidence. You need a place to put
  your part of the weight too.

And if they ever talk about not wanting to be here, or you are scared for
their safety, treat it as urgent: stay with them if you can and help them to
real help — a crisis helpline (the help button in this app lists one) or
emergency services. Carers are allowed to call for backup. Backup is the
plan, not a failure.
