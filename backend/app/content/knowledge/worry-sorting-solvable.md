Worries feel like one big storm until you sort them. On paper, they split
into two piles, and each pile needs a different response.

**Pile one: something I can act on.**
"What if I fail the test because I missed a topic?" → action: check the
syllabus tonight. For these, write the very next small step and, when you
can, do it. A worry with a next step becomes a task, and tasks are calmer
than storms.

**Pile two: nothing I can do right now.**
"What if the results are bad?" "What if they're angry at me?" If the honest
answer is "nothing I can do today", the worry is a what-if, not a task.
What-ifs are the mind rehearsing for things that have not happened. They
respond better to being named than argued with: "That is a what-if. I am
doing what I can. The rest is not mine to carry today."

Some rules of thumb:

- "What if..." questions usually land in pile two.
- If a worry keeps returning to pile one with the same step, that is your
  mind telling you the step matters. Do it, or put it in your diary.
- Sorting counts as doing something. You are not avoiding the worry; you are
  giving each one the response that actually fits it.
