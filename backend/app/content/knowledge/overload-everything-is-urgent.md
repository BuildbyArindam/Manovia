Some weeks hand you five deadlines, three promises and two small crises at
once. Everything screams. The screams are lying — nothing improves when you
try to answer all of them simultaneously. The move is not to go faster; it
is to line them up.

**One minute of triage, on paper.** List every open thing without
censoring. Then ask two questions per item:

- What actually happens if this slips a day? A week? (Real consequences are
  usually smaller and slower than the scream.)
- Whose clock is it on — mine, or someone else's anxiety?

**Sort into three piles, not ten.**

1. *Due soon, real consequences* — do these first, in order of date.
2. *Important but not today* — put each on a named day in your calendar.
   Named days quiet items down; "later" does not.
3. *Not actually mine* — borrowed urgency, someone else's panic, things you
   said yes to while feeling guilty. Renegotiate, decline, or let them go.

**Tell the truth to the people waiting.** "I have X and Y on Friday; I can
give you Z on Monday" is respected far more often than feared. Silence is
what turns one waiting person into three anxious messages.

**Then work in one lane.** Multitasking is how the overload feeling is
manufactured. One task, one timer, phone in a drawer — then the next.

Overwhelm is usually a queue problem, not a you problem. Queues can be
ordered; you cannot outrun them all at once.
