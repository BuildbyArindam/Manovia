A common trap: "I'll do it when I feel like it." With low mood, feeling like
it may not come for days — and the longer nothing happens, the heavier
everything grows. Mood tends to follow action more reliably than it leads it.

The pattern that works looks backwards to most of us:

- **Not:** feel ready → do the thing → feel better.
- **Actually:** do a small version of the thing → mood lifts a little → a
  bit more feels possible.

So the skill is starting before you want to — while being kind about it:

- **Shrink the start.** Not "clean the room" but "put the mugs in the
  kitchen". Starting is the expensive part; momentum is cheap afterwards.
- **Book the thing, then let the booked thing carry you.** A plan with a
  friend at 6 pm gets you out even at 5.30, when the sofa is winning.
- **Count contact, not completion.** Ten minutes at the desk counts. Reading
  one page counts. You are rebuilding the habit of doing, not clearing a
  backlog today.
- **Notice the after-feeling.** Once it is done, pause and register that it
  is done. That small trace of "that went better than expected" is the
  evidence your brain needs to make starting easier next time.

You are not lazy. Wanting things and doing things are run by different
machinery, and low mood disconnects them. Acting first is how you wire them
back together.
