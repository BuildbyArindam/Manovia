You can do this anywhere — in an exam hall, on a bus, in a corridor — and it
takes about a minute. Nobody around you will notice.

1. **Press your feet into the floor.** Feel the ground holding you. Wiggle
   your toes if you can.
2. **Breathe out slowly**, longer than the breath in. Let your shoulders drop
   as you do.
3. **Look around and name three things** you can see, silently: "chair.
   Window. Door."
4. **Unclench** somewhere you hold tightness — your jaw, your hands, your
   forehead.

That is the whole exercise. It works by giving your attention a simple, real
task while the wave of feeling passes. Feelings rise, peak, and settle, and
small anchors help you ride the peak without making it bigger.

Some people repeat it several times across a hard day. Practising it when
things are calm makes it easier to reach for when things are not.
