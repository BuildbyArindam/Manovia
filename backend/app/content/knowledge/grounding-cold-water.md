Strong feelings can sweep you somewhere you did not choose to go. A sharp,
harmless sensation can cut through the spiral and bring you back to now.

Things people try:

- Hold a cold glass of water, or run cold water over your wrists.
- Splash cool water on your face and breathe out slowly.
- Step outside and let cold air hit your face for a few breaths.
- Hold an ice cube in your hand until you can focus on it fully — keep it
  comfortable, never painful.

While you do it, say what you are doing in plain words: "This is cold water. I
am in my kitchen. It is Tuesday." Naming the ordinary moment reminds your body
that right now, in this second, you are safe enough.

This is a reset for the moment, not a fix for what caused the feeling. Once you
are steadier, it may help to write down what happened, or tell someone you
trust.

If strong sensations make you feel worse rather than better, skip this one.
Softer anchors — feet on the floor, a slow breath out — are just as valid.
