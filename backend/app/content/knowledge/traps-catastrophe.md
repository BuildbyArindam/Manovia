Your mind is a brilliant disaster-film writer. One unsettled feeling, and it
has a full script: the mistake, the consequences, the ending, the review.

It sounds like:

- "My heart skipped a beat — something must be wrong with me."
- "I stumbled in that conversation. Now they all think I'm strange."
- "If I fail this exam, my whole future is gone."

The mind does this to protect you: it rehearses disasters so nothing catches
you out. But it treats the worst ending as the *likely* ending, and that is
almost never true.

A way to check the script:

- **Separate the event from the story.** Event: "I stumbled in a
  conversation." Story: "they all think I'm strange." Which part do you
  actually know?
- **Ask for odds.** Honestly — how often have your disaster predictions come
  true? Most people's rate is low. Your mind keeps the one hit and forgets
  the hundred misses.
- **Write the boring version.** "I stumbled, someone noticed for a second,
  we carried on." Boring versions are usually the accurate ones.
- **Ask "and then what?" three times.** The future is not one door; it has
  corridors, second chances and people who help. Worst-case scripts rely on
  everything after the disaster also going wrong.

Preparing a little for a hard outcome is sensible. Living inside the worst
moment before it happens is just suffering twice.
