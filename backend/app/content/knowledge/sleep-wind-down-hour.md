Sleep rarely arrives on command. What helps more is a gentle slope towards
it: an hour before bed where the day winds down instead of ending at full
speed.

A wind-down hour can be small and even a little boring — that is the point:

- Dim the lights where you are. Bright light late in the evening tells your
  body clock it is still daytime.
- Park the heavy conversations, the arguments and the news scrolling for
  tomorrow, on purpose. They will still be there in the morning, and you will
  meet them better rested.
- Do the same two or three quiet things in the same order: a wash, tomorrow's
  clothes out, a page of something light. A familiar sequence is a cue that
  says "safe to stop now".
- If your bed is where you study or argue with yourself all day, it becomes a
  desk. If you can, keep the bed for sleeping, and let your brain relearn
  what it means.

You will not manage a perfect wind-down every night, and you do not need to.
Most nights, mostly, is plenty.

If you are lying awake far longer than it takes to fall asleep, it is fine to
get up, sit somewhere dim, and do something quiet until you feel drowsy.
