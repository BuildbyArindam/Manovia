The exam is over but the waiting stretches on, and the not-knowing does its
own kind of damage. Days feel like a held breath.

Ways to let the breath out a little:

- **Limit the replay.** Re-reading the syllabus to guess what you got wrong
  is your mind begging for certainty it cannot have. When the replay starts,
  name it: "checking again" — and put your attention somewhere with a
  beginning and an end, like a show, a game, a walk.
- **Plan for both versions, once.** Briefly and on paper: if it goes well, I
  will do this; if it goes badly, I will do that, and who I would talk to.
  Then you have already met the bad version, and it loses some of its shadow.
- **Keep the days shaped.** Fixed wake time, one thing to do, one thing to
  look forward to. Waiting time with no shape is where dread grows.
- **Say it to one person.** "I keep thinking about results day" out loud is
  smaller than the same sentence circling in your head.

Whatever the result, it is information about one performance on one day. It
is not a measure of your worth, and it is not the last door. If the waiting
is crushing your days, tell someone — you do not have to carry it alone until
the date arrives.
