If worries ambush you all day, one of the strangest-sounding — and most
effective — tricks is to give them an appointment instead of fighting each
one as it arrives.

How it works:

1. **Pick a slot.** Same time each day, fifteen or twenty minutes, not near
   bedtime, and somewhere you will not be interrupted. "Worry time: 5.30 pm,
   kitchen table."
2. **When a worry shows up outside the slot**, note it in a few words — on
   paper or in your phone — and tell yourself: "Noted. That is for 5.30."
   Then bring your attention back to what you were doing.
3. **Keep the appointment.** In the slot, worry deliberately. Go through your
   list. Think hard, write, even pace. Worrying on purpose is allowed in
   here.
4. **Close the slot.** When it ends, fold the paper, close the notebook, and
   do something that changes the channel — a short walk, a shower, music.

What most people notice: half the noted worries look stale by 5.30. They
borrowed their urgency from the moment, and the moment passed.

The worries that still matter get real attention — in the slot, where you
can think properly instead of flinching all day. And worry time is a skill
that grows on you: it can take a week or two of keeping the appointment
before your mind learns the worries really will be heard later.
