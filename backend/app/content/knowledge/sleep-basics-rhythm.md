Your body sleeps best when it can predict you. You do not need a perfect
schedule — a rough rhythm is enough to work with.

- **Keep wake-up times steady**, even after a bad night and even at weekends.
  A regular waking time is the anchor the rest of the day's sleepiness hangs
  from.
- **Get daylight early.** Ten minutes outside in the morning, or by a window,
  tells your body clock when the day started. It makes evening sleepiness
  arrive on time.
- **Watch the late caffeine.** Tea, coffee, cola and energy drinks can linger
  for hours. If sleep is patchy, try keeping caffeine to the morning for a
  week and see what changes.
- **Eat something at sensible times.** A very late, heavy meal can compete
  with sleep; going to bed very hungry can too.
- **Naps are allowed**, just short and early — twenty minutes after lunch,
  not three hours at sunset.

Nights will still go wrong sometimes. That is normal and it recovers. If you
have changed what you can and sleep is still broken most nights for weeks,
that is a good thing to bring to a doctor or counsellor.
