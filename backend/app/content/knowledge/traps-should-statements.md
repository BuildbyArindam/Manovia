"Should" sounds like motivation. Often it is a small whip.

It sounds like:

- "I should be able to handle this without struggling."
- "I should be studying every evening like others do."
- "I shouldn't feel sad — my life is fine."

Here is the problem: "should" judges you against an imaginary standard, then
punishes the gap. It rarely moves you forward, because shame is a poor
engine. People do not study harder, sleep earlier or feel better by
whipping themselves; they get more drained, and more avoidant.

A more workable swap:

- **"Should" → "I'd like to" or "It would help if".** "I should study" → "It
  would help me to do one hour tonight." Same goal, none of the shame, and
  it is now a choice you can actually start.
- **Check whose standard it is.** Should, compared to whom? Often the rule
  came from a highlight reel, an old expectation, or a cousin who never
  existed the way you imagine them.
- **Test the rule kindly.** "I should handle this alone" — says who? Would
  you say a friend *should* handle this alone, no help, ever?
- **Keep the value, drop the whip.** Under nearly every "should" is
  something you care about. Keep the caring part; release the punishing
  part.

Standards are useful. Standards that only make you feel worse and never
change your behaviour are just weight. Put those down.
