When your mood is low, everything asks too much of you. Even small tasks look
like mountains, so nothing gets done, and then you feel worse about not doing
— a loop that tightens. One way out of the loop is not waiting to feel
better. It is starting absurdly small.

Pick something so small it is almost embarrassing:

- Sit up and put both feet on the floor.
- Drink a glass of water.
- Open the window for one minute.
- Put three things back where they belong.
- Walk to the end of the street and back.

Then stop, notice you did it, and let that be the whole victory. Not "only
that" — *that*. You moved while it was hard. That is exactly the muscle this
builds.

Two hints that make it work better:

- **Choose the step before you are in the moment.** Decide tonight what
  tomorrow's tiny step is, so low-mood-you does not have to negotiate.
- **Keep the bar low on purpose** until it feels easy. Then the next step
  grows by itself: end of the street becomes the shop; three things become
  ten minutes of tidying.

Movement comes first; the wanting usually limps in afterwards. And if weeks
have gone by where even tiny steps feel impossible most days, that is worth
telling a professional about — not because you failed at this, but because
you should not have to carry it alone.
