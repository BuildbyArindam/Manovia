Stress rarely arrives without notice. It sends small signals first — we just
miss them because we are busy. Learning *your* early signs turns "sudden
meltdown" into "ah, time to slow down for a day".

Common ones — you will have your own:

- **Body:** jaw or shoulders tight, headaches, stomach in knots, feeling
  restless or heavy, getting ill every deadline season.
- **Sleep:** hard to fall asleep, or waking at 4 a.m. with the day already
  in your chest.
- **Behaviour:** snapping at small things, skipping meals, cancelling plans,
  scrolling for hours, more tea/coffee than usual.
- **Mind:** reading the same paragraph four times, small tasks feeling big,
  losing your sense of humour.

Try this once: think of a hard week recently, and write down the first three
things that changed. Those three are your dashboard lights.

Then attach an action to each one, in advance:

- Jaw tight → two minutes of slow breathing with a long breath out.
- Skipping meals → today, eat actual food at actual times, however simple.
- Snapping → ten minutes outside alone before you reply to anyone.

Catching stress early is not weakness. It is maintenance — the same reason
you charge your phone before it dies rather than after.
