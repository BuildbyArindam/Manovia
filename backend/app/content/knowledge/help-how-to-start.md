Deciding to seek help is the hard part — well done for reaching it. The
steps after it are smaller than they look. Here is the path, made concrete.

**Find your door.** Depending on where you are and what you can access: a
campus or school counsellor, a family doctor, a community health centre, a
workplace support service, or a helpline that can point you to local
options. If you are unsure where to begin, a helpline is a good map: the
help button in this app can show you one.

**Use the words you already have.** You do not need a story with a
beginning, middle and end. This is enough: "I've been struggling with my
mood/sleep/stress for a few weeks and I'd like some help." Practice it once
out loud; it gets easier.

**Expect simple questions.** Sleep, appetite, mood, how long, how it affects
your days. Honest plain answers are perfect. It is okay to say "I don't
know" and okay to bring notes.

**Book it like an appointment it is.** Write the number down, send the
email, tell a friend the day so it becomes real. First-available slots can
have a wait — put your name down anyway, and use the wait to keep your
routines going.

**Bring one trusted person along** if the first step scares you. Sitting
next to someone in a waiting room is a time-honoured technology.

If cost, language or location is the barrier, say so when you call — many
services have sliding fees, phone options, or languages you would not guess.
The worst outcome of asking is usually a "no, but try this", which is still a
door, not a wall.
